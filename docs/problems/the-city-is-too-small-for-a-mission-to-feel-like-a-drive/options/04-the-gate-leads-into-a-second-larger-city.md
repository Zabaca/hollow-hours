## Solution

### The shape

- **Chapter 1 is today's game, unchanged**: the quarter, its three missions, its gate. Its ending keeps the roll-out, first light, Vera's and Ruck's closing lines and the camera drawing back from the stopped car. Then, instead of the credits, a card: "Chapter 2. The next night." The screen fades and the harbour city loads, at night.
- **Chapter 2 is the harbour city** (a working name for the owner to change): a second walled city, built fresh, three new missions, and the credits at its end.
- **One city is loaded at a time.** The cut at the gate is the only crossing; nothing is driven between the two. Continue, a retry, a wreck in free roam and Restart all happen in the city the story is in.

### Chapter 2's story (a sketch for the owner to approve or change)

At first light the cordon burned the quarter behind them. Ruck's convoy took Red, Vera and Teo across the river to the harbour city, where the cordon kept its camp. By the next dusk the camp's radio had gone quiet: the Gaunts came up out of the harbour city's drains too. Vera runs the radio from the roof of a school the cordon made a field hospital; Teo works inside it; Ruck is out on the ring road with what is left of his unit. Red is still silent.

| # | Mission | One line |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Dead Convoy** (rescue) | Chapter 2 opens on the ring road at the city's east edge: Ruck's truck went off the road on the far side of the city at dusk, so drive to it, hold while he drags himself in, and bring him to the field hospital. |
| 5 | **Floodlights** (delivery, three stops) | The hospital's floodlights are all that keeps the pack off it and its generators are failing: take fuel from the dockside depot to three generator posts in three districts before the lights die. |
| 6 | **The Lift Bridge** (escape) | The only road out is the lift bridge at the harbour mouth, and it is up: carry Vera, Teo and Ruck to its control house, hold out while Teo brings it down, and cross before it lifts again; first light on the far bank, and the credits. |

They reuse what the story can already do: reach a place, hold still at one, run Gaunts down, stay alive, a timer, and a gate-like crossing that opens. Two new ways to fail come with them: the floodlights going out, and the bridge lifting before you are across.

**The new radio lines**: about 24. That is about seven for each mission, a call that opens the chapter, two new failure lines, and the closing lines at the bridge, about 1,600 characters with their cue words. At the rate measured so far (631 credits for chapter 1's 21 lines and four effects) that is about 750 credits, plus up to about 200 for three new effects (the bridge's horn, its motors, a generator dying). The cap is 1,500 credits. It needs the owner's yes on the script and the spend; until the lines are bought, every line shows as a subtitle for its reading time, as chapter 1's did, so everything else is built and played first.

### The harbour city

- **How big**: about three times as wide and as tall as the quarter, about 400 by 300 m, 36 blocks of today's size on a street grid like today's (roads every 64 m east to west and 48 m north to south, 14 m wide). The size is one setting, so the city can grow later.

| City | Size | Blocks | A mission leg by road | At 60 km/h | At 90 km/h |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The quarter (chapter 1) | 140 by 108 m | 4 | 90 to 131 m (straight line) | 5 to 8 s | 4 to 5 s |
| **About 3 times (recommended)** | **about 400 by 300 m** | **36** | **about 300 to 560 m** | **19 to 34 s** | **12 to 22 s** |
| About 4 times | about 520 by 400 m | 64 | about 410 to 740 m | 25 to 44 s | 16 to 30 s |
| About 5 times | about 650 by 490 m | 100 | about 510 to 920 m | 31 to 55 s | 20 to 37 s |

  The leg lengths are estimates along the street grid with the places spread to the corners, before any roadblock; the mission test measures the real ones. A city two to three times wider gives legs of about 20 to 35 seconds; 30 to 60 seconds needs about five times. Three times is recommended for this round: it is 36 blocks to build and keep fast rather than 64 or 100, every leg is three to four times as long as chapter 1's, and growing it later is a setting and moving the places out (about 2 to 3 agent hours).
- **The block kit.** Each block is filled from a kit of block types, chosen and varied from a seed so no two look alike, in the quarter's look (the same materials, windows, signs, awnings, lamps and wrecks): towers with lit windows here and there, brick rows of three to five storeys with shopfronts, warehouses and fenced yards with containers, low houses with yards and dead trees, empty lots with burning barrels, a parking structure. Shop signs use plain words that are ours (LAUNDRY, HARDWARE, NOODLES, BAKERY, CINEMA and the like).
- **The districts**: the docks along the south (cranes, containers, the fuel depot), the river or canal through the west with three bridges and the lift bridge at the harbour mouth, the old town of brick rows in the north, towers in the centre, the school that is now the field hospital in the south-east, the ring road round the edge where the convoy died.
- **The places** spread to the far sides so every leg crosses the city: the chapter's start on the ring road (east edge), Ruck's truck (far north-west), the field hospital (south-east, Vera on its roof), the fuel depot (south-west docks), the three generator posts (north, centre-west, east), the bridge's control house and the lift bridge (far west). Every mission leg is aimed at 300 m or more by road and the run to the bridge at 450 m or more.
- **Everything is original**: no names, maps or assets from any other game, film or real city.

### What happens on a long drive

At 60 km/h on a straight the pack is near-harmless; its danger sits at corners, stops and crashes. So the danger is put where the car has to slow:

1. **Roadblocks and squeezes.** Wreck barricades, a truck jackknifed across a street and police barriers cut the straight routes. Every mission leg's shortest way crosses at least one. It either forces a turn into a side street, or leaves a gap about a car and a half wide that takes the car down to about 30 km/h.
2. **Ambushes at the squeezes and bridges.** Two or three Gaunts wait in the dark beside a roadblock, a squeeze or a bridge end, out of the headlights, and come at the car from the side as it slows within about 35 m. Each mission leg has one or two; in free roam a few are armed at random, so a known route is not a safe one.
3. **The pack follows the car.** New Gaunts come in 60 to 120 m from the car, ahead of it and toward where it is going, never in its headlights. One left more than 150 m behind that is not hunting is quietly dropped and replaced ahead. In the quarter this changes almost nothing, because the quarter is barely wider than that band (an inference).
4. **Dark stretches and lit refuges.** About one lamp in five lives, gathered at a few junctions; the floodlit hospital is a refuge until mission 5's lights fail.

The pack's own numbers (its size, kill speeds, the pounce) are unchanged; they are tuned from the owner's hand play.

### Finding the way

- **A route on the radar and the full map**: a faint amber line along the streets to the objective, round the roadblocks.
- **The radar widens with speed**: about 60 m to its rim when crawling, about 110 m flat out.
- **The full map** shows whichever city you are in, with its places, river, bridges and roadblocks.

### What else changes

- **Saves and Continue.** The save still holds only mission names; the next mission says which city to load. Finishing chapter 1 saves with chapter 2's first mission next, so Continue opens the harbour city on its ring road. A save made before this change that had finished the story is read as "chapter 2 next", not set aside as damaged. The title's Continue names the chapter and mission; "The end" moves to after chapter 2.
- **The ending.** Chapter 1 ends at first light with the card and the cut; the credits roll at the far end of the lift bridge, ending on a new last line for the owner to approve (today's is "Red got out.").
- **Survive the Night.** It is still unlocked by escaping the quarter and still plays there. Once chapter 2 is finished, the title also offers it in the harbour city.
- **Tests and the autopilot.** Every test of chapter 1 keeps running on the quarter unchanged. The autopilot learns to read whichever city it is in, and drives chapter 2 in the tests.
- **Frame rate and download size.** Only one city is in memory at a time, so the quarter costs nothing in chapter 2. The harbour city stays smooth by drawing only what can be seen: each block is its own piece the renderer can skip, the camera stops drawing where the fog has hidden everything, and buildings hide what is behind them. This is aimed at, and measured on the Mac from slice 2. The download grows by the harbour city's model (about 15 MB before compression, scaled from the quarter's 2.2 MB by area; an inference) and about 24 voiced lines (about 1.5 MB). The Mac build is aimed near 75 to 80 MB (66 MB today).

### The detail pass (optional, last)

Tiling textures for brick, concrete, plaster, asphalt and pavement, made in Blender from its own procedural textures (as the creature's skin was, so free and ours), bevelled building edges, window frames and sills, and more props (roof units, drainpipes, phone poles with sagging wires, bins and bags, bus stops, chain-link fences, cones), all in the block kit so they land on every block of the harbour city. The quarter keeps its look unless the owner asks for the same pass there (about an hour more).

## Trade-offs

- **A second city rather than a bigger quarter** (option 01, which the owner turned away from). The quarter he knows stays as it is and becomes a short, tight opening; the long drives live where the story goes next. It costs a chapter of new story and new voice lines, which a bigger quarter would not have needed.
- **A cut at the gate rather than a road between** (option 05). 4 to 6 agent hours less, and no long straight road where a fast car outruns the pack. The cost is that the journey between the cities is told by a card, not driven.
- **One city loaded at a time rather than both.** Simple, light on memory and on frame time; the price is a load at the cut (a few seconds, to be measured).
- **A grid rather than an irregular street plan.** The simulation's walls stay square to the map, as the car's collisions (ported from Roadkill and checked against it) need, and the time roughly halves. An irregular plan (a diagonal boulevard, a bending river road) was option 03; it would need collisions with angled walls, new code with nothing to check it against.
- **A kit of block types rather than copies of the quarter's four blocks** (option 02's idea). About 2 to 3 agent hours more for a city that does not repeat itself.
- **Three times the quarter rather than four or five.** Legs of 19 to 34 seconds at 60 km/h, short of the 30 to 60 once suggested; in exchange, half or a third of the blocks, and less risk to the frame rate and download. Growing it later is a setting.
- **Danger at the squeezes rather than retuning the pack.** The pack's tested behaviour stays; the city does the work. If straights still feel safe in the hand play, the mid-leap kill speed is the lever (docs/follow-ups.md).
- **A route line on the radar.** It helps in a city eight times the quarter's area and is what open-world driving games do; it also makes getting lost less likely, which some would call part of the dread.

## User Stories

1. As a player, I want to drive out of the quarter's gate into somewhere new, so that escaping means going on, not stopping.
2. As a player, I want the quarter to stay as I learned it, so that chapter 1 is still the tight opening I played.
3. As a player, I want a card that tells me a day has passed, so that night falling again makes sense after first light.
4. As a player, I want chapter 2's mission drives to take tens of seconds, so that a mission feels like a drive across a city.
5. As a player, I want the same voices on the radio in chapter 2, so that it is the same story going on.
6. As a player, I want the harbour city to look varied, so that I can tell where I am without the map.
7. As a player, I want roadblocks that make me choose a way round or squeeze through slowly, so that a route is a decision.
8. As a player, I want Gaunts to come at me from the side where I have to slow down, so that a long drive has danger in it.
9. As a player, I want the pack to find me wherever I am, so that no part of the city is safe.
10. As a player, I want a route on the radar and the map, so that I can drive fast without getting lost.
11. As a player, I want the radar to show further when I am going fast, so that I see the next turn in time.
12. As a player, I want the full map to show the city I am in, so that the places it names are the ones around me.
13. As a player with a save, I want Continue to open the city my next mission is in, so that I pick up where I stopped.
14. As a player who finished the story before chapter 2 existed, I want my save to carry on into chapter 2, so that I do not have to replay chapter 1.
15. As a player, I want the credits after the lift bridge, so that the game ends where the story does.
16. As a player who has finished chapter 2, I want Survive the Night in either city, so that I can play the old round in the new place.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Cities are data.** Each city has an id, its generated layout and its model. The quarter's stay as they are. The harbour city's come from a new generator script beside today's city script, which shares its materials and helpers (moved into a shared module both scripts use) and adds the block kit, the landmarks, the river, its bridges and the lift bridge, the roadblocks, and a size setting. Each block, with its pavement and its stretch of road, is its own model piece. The layout gains the roads as a list, the roadblocks, the bridges and the ambush spots, beside today's records; the lift bridge is the harbour city's gate record (a position, width and direction), so the gate logic that exists serves it.
- **The story knows cities.** Every place and every mission names its city. A chapter is the run of missions in one city. The mission state machine reports a new event when a chapter's last mission is done, and the game scene answers it with the card, a save, and a scene change to the next city. The story-complete event, the ending's credits and "The end" belong to the last chapter only.
- **The game scene** takes the city to load from the session, set by the title (from the save's next mission), by the cut, or by a flag (a new `--city=` and the existing `--mission=` reaching missions 4 to 6). The layout, the model, the map and the camera all read that city. Survive the Night takes a city too.
- **The save** keeps its shape. The save check accepts up to the story's full list of missions, and reads a version-1 save that finished the three missions of chapter 1 (next empty, the ending seen) as "chapter 2 next". The unlocks file gains the harbour city's Survive the Night.
- **The world answers from buckets.** The simulation's world sorts its boxes, posts and lights into cells of about 16 m. Every question (the car's collisions, pushing a creature out of a wall, line of sight, is a point lit, how far a point is from a wall) looks only at the cells it touches. The answers stay the same as today's full loops.
- **The path grid** marks each box's and post's own cells instead of testing every cell against every box, giving the same solid cells. **The camera's wall test** asks the world's buckets instead of copying every box each frame.
- **The pack's director** places new Gaunts 60 to 120 m from the car (today: any hiding place 60 m or more away), preferring those ahead of it and toward its objective, never lit or in the headlights. One that is not hunting or holding on and is more than 150 m behind the car is removed and replaced ahead.
- **Ambushes are story data.** An ambush spot is a place and a count. A step's effects can arm one, and a step's timed effects gain a trigger by distance to a place as well as by seconds. When the car comes within about 35 m of an armed spot, its Gaunts are placed out of the headlights and begin hunting. In free roam a few spots are armed at random from the layout's list.
- **Chapter 2's missions** are rows in the story data like chapter 1's: the places, the steps (reach, hold, survive, the crossing), the floodlights timer and the bridge timer with their failure reasons and lines, the pack sizes, the alerts. The lines go into the same radio script with their speakers, so the subtitles and the voices can never disagree.
- **The lift bridge's view** is a sibling of the west gate's: the bridge's two decks come down instead of two leaves sliding apart, with lamps that go from red to green and a horn. The escape rolls the car across to rest on the far bank, as it rolls out of the gate today.
- **The autopilot** reads the arena from the world, plans on its own path grid across whichever city it is in, and does its run-down passes along the street at the objective's place (not the avenue's fixed coordinates; this closes that follow-up).
- **The map** draws roads, the river, bridges and roadblocks from the current city's layout and the gate or bridge from its record. The radar's range follows the car's speed. A route from the car's path grid to the objective is drawn on the radar and the map.
- **The voices** go through the existing voice generator: a dry run prints the character count, and one run refuses more than 1,500 characters without the explicit flag. The new effects go through the effects generator. The credits log takes a line for each purchase.

## Testing Decisions

- **The seam stays the one the tests use today**: step the game simulation and read its events, plus the layout data for checks on places. No new seam.
- **Chapter 1 is unchanged**: the car, pack, mission, presenter, settings, menu, radio and story-input tests run on the quarter as they do today.
- **The bucket world gives the same answers as the full loops** on the quarter, for a sweep of points and lines; **the path grid** built the new way has the same solid cells.
- **A step costs no more in the big city**: the harbour city's step with a full pack takes no more time than the quarter's does (recorded before and after).
- **The pack follows**: after a 60-second drive across the harbour city, the live pack is within 150 m of the car, and new Gaunts appear 60 to 120 m away, out of the light.
- **Every chapter 2 leg by road**: each leg is at least 300 m on the car's path grid and the run to the bridge at least 450 m; every place is inside the arena, clear of everything with the car's clearance, and reachable from the chapter's start; each leg's shortest route crosses a roadblock or a bridge.
- **The chapter boundary**: finishing mission 3 reports the end of chapter 1 (not the end of the story), asks for a save with mission 4 next, and leaves the credits for later. A save with mission 4 next loads the harbour city. An old save that finished chapter 1 continues into chapter 2. Finishing mission 6 reports the end of the story and the credits.
- **Ambushes**: an armed spot places its Gaunts when the car comes within range and not before, out of the headlights; a spot not armed does nothing.
- **Chapter 2, driven**: the autopilot plays missions 4 to 6 with the pack off within a time bound. Each new failure (the floodlights, the bridge) fails its mission with its reason and line.
- **Frame time, load time and download size**: from slice 2 on, the builder logs frame time and draw calls on one fixed autopilot drive in the harbour city (High and Low), the time the cut takes, and the Mac build's size. These are reported, not asserted.
- **Screenshots** (the builder's own): the card, each district, a roadblock squeeze, a bridge, the lift bridge up and down, the full map in each city, the radar with the route, the credits after the bridge.

## Out of Scope

- Changing the quarter, its missions or its lines.
- A road driven between the cities (option 05).
- More than three missions in chapter 2, side jobs, people on foot, new cars or creature kinds.
- Retuning the pack's own numbers.
- An irregular street plan, height (ramps, overpasses), interiors.
- The key art (its own branch).
